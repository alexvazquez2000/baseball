Standard accounting codes, also known as a chart of accounts, are a system of numbering and naming accounts used to classify and track financial transactions within an organization. These codes help ensure accurate financial reporting by categorizing transactions into assets, liabilities, equity, revenue, and expenses. While there isn't a universal standard, most organizations use a similar structure based on these categories. Here's a breakdown of the common categories and example codes:

1. Assets (typically numbered 1000-1999): These represent what a company owns, such as cash, accounts receivable, inventory, and property, plant, and equipment.
Cash: 1000
Accounts Receivable: 1100
Inventory: 1200
Equipment: 1400
Land: 1500

2. Liabilities (typically numbered 2000-2999): These represent what a company owes to others, such as accounts payable, loans, and deferred revenue.
Accounts Payable: 2000
Loans Payable: 2200
Deferred Revenue: 2400

3. Equity (typically numbered 3000-3999): This represents the owner's stake in the company, including retained earnings, contributed capital, and other equity accounts.
Retained Earnings: 3000
Common Stock: 3200
Additional Paid-In Capital: 3400

4. Revenue (typically numbered 4000-4999): This represents income generated from the company's primary operations, such as sales revenue, service revenue, and interest income.
Sales Revenue: 4000
Service Revenue: 4200
Interest Income: 4400

5. Expenses (typically numbered 5000-5999): This represents the costs incurred in the process of generating revenue, such as cost of goods sold, salaries, rent, utilities, and depreciation.
Cost of Goods Sold: 5000
Salaries Expense: 5200
Rent Expense: 5400
Utilities Expense: 5600
Depreciation Expense: 5800

These account codes are linked to specific General Ledger (G/L) accounts, which are used to allocate expenses to the appropriate cost centers, projects, or departments. They are also used in conjunction with Concur Expense, a system that helps manage and track employee expenses, to ensure that expense data is correctly applied and accounted for in the financial records.
